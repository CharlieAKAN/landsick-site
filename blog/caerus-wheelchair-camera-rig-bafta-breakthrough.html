A Wheelchair Camera Rig Gets a BAFTA Boost

The news

BAFTA named its 2026 Breakthrough UK cohort on November 4, and one name should make filmmakers stop and look twice: Chris Lynch, presenter and producer of In the Driving Seat.

Lynch is also the inventor of CAERUS, reported as the world’s first wheelchair-integrated professional camera system. It is a cinema rig built into a self-balancing wheelchair platform. It can move up to 20 km/h, rotate 360 degrees, and carry cinema-grade camera setups.

This is not just a nice inclusion story. It is a camera movement story. It is a set access story. It is a new tool story.

Why we care

On set, movement is power. Who gets to move the camera shapes the scene. Who gets to operate that camera shapes the work. For years, too many disabled filmmakers have had to fight the gear before they could even fight for the shot.

CAERUS flips that. It turns the wheelchair into the dolly, the track, the remote head, and the operator’s seat. That means low tracking shots, orbit moves, live broadcast work, and controlled motion can come from a system built around the person using it.

For us, that is the big lesson. Better access can also mean better images. When the tool is made with real bodies in mind, the camera language gets wider.

The details that matter

Digital Camera World reports that Lynch began developing CAERUS in 2020 and patented the design in 2025. The system works with professional cameras, including RED and Sony setups, through a side mount and gimbal or Steadicam-style build.

The official CAERUS site says the platform is mechanically and electronically stabilized, wirelessly operated, built in the UK, and already working on broadcast productions. It also lists three setups: location and drama, studio and multicam, and outside broadcast and live.

That range matters. A tool like this is not only for one kind of shoot. It can live in docs, commercials, TV, studio work, live events, and narrative film.

The BAFTA boost

BAFTA Breakthrough is built to give rising screen talent support, meetings, career guidance, networking, and BAFTA membership. The 2026 UK cohort includes 21 creatives across film, TV, documentary, unscripted, and games, with Netflix supporting the programme.

That puts Lynch and CAERUS closer to the rooms where gear choices, hiring choices, and production habits get made.

And that is the real headline. This rig does not need pity. It needs demos. It needs producers, DPs, rental houses, and studios to see what it can do when it is treated like a creative tool, not a side note.

What filmmakers should take from this

We talk a lot about new cameras, new lenses, new AI tools, and new workflows. But the future of filmmaking is not just sharper images. It is more people getting to make them.

CAERUS is a reminder that access is not a charity lane. It is a design challenge. And when we solve design challenges well, the whole set gets better.

If you are a producer, ask who your set is built for. If you are a DP, ask what movement you are missing because the gear has been too narrow. If you are a director, ask who has not been able to stand behind the camera yet.

Sometimes the next great shot does not come from a new sensor. Sometimes it comes from finally building the rig around the filmmaker.
