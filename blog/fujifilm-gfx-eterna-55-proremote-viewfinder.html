Fujifilm Makes ETERNA More Set-Ready

Fujifilm Is Thinking Like a Film Crew

On September 8, 2026, Fujifilm announced two updates for the GFX ETERNA 55 filmmaking camera: the ETERNA ProRemote app and the VF-GFXC2 electronic viewfinder. That may sound like a small gear update. It is not. This is about making a large-format cinema camera easier to use when the camera is rigged, boxed in, high up, low to the ground, or buried inside a tight setup.

The big idea is simple: less fighting the camera, more making the shot.

Why The ProRemote App Matters

ETERNA ProRemote lets crews control the GFX ETERNA 55 from an iPhone or iPad. It connects by Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, or wired LAN. Fujifilm says it supports three connection modes, from simple single-camera control to multi-camera setups with up to four cameras.

That is useful on real sets. Think car rigs, crane shots, tabletop work, small stages, live capture, or any setup where touching the camera is either slow or risky. Instead of sending a crew member in to tap through menus, we can adjust key settings from a device nearby.

For lean crews, this is even bigger. A director, DP, or tech can monitor and make changes without adding more bodies around the camera. That keeps the set calmer. Calmer sets usually make better images.

The Viewfinder Is Built For Open Gate

Fujifilm also announced the VF-GFXC2, a dedicated EVF for the GFX ETERNA 55. It has a 2048 x 1536 display, up to 1000 nits of brightness, and a native 4:3 OLED panel made to show the camera’s open-gate image without wasting the frame.

That 4:3 part matters. Open gate is not just a buzzword. It gives us more image area to frame, protect for different aspect ratios, or work with anamorphic lenses. But if the monitor or viewfinder does not show that space cleanly, the tool gets in the way. Fujifilm is trying to keep the full frame visible, clear, and usable.

This Is The Direction Cinema Cameras Are Moving

Camera bodies are not enough anymore. The camera has to fit the workflow. We need clean monitoring, fast control, strong rigging options, and tools that let small crews move like big ones.

That is why this update is worth watching. Fujifilm is not only selling a large-format look. They are building the support system around it. The app is set for late September 2026. The VF-GFXC2 is expected by the end of 2026, and Fujifilm is showing it at IBC 2026 in Amsterdam from September 11 to 14.

What We Can Learn From It

For us as filmmakers, the lesson is clear: the best camera is not always the one with the biggest spec sheet. It is the one that helps the crew move faster, see better, and stay focused on the story.

If a tool saves five minutes on every setup, that adds up. If it keeps the camera operator from guessing on an open-gate frame, that adds up too. Good workflow is not boring. Good workflow is how we protect the creative work.
