Melbourne Becomes New York

The news

This week, a real production story caught our eye: Jamie Foxx is heading to Melbourne to shoot Deadlocked, a new Amazon MGM action thriller. The film starts production in early October, with Melbourne standing in for New York City.

That is not just a fun headline. It is a clean reminder that filmmaking is often about one big trick: making one place feel like another place.

Why this matters

Deadlocked will film across Melbourne’s CBD, Docklands, Port Melbourne, and Docklands Studios Melbourne. The production is expected to bring in more than $36 million for Victoria and create more than 855 local cast and crew jobs, plus roles for 1,000 extras.

For a large studio film, that is the kind of move that makes sense. You get strong crews, usable streets, studio space, and a city that can play another city on camera.

For the rest of us, the lesson is smaller, but still powerful. Your location does not have to be perfect. It has to be believable.

The filmmaker lesson

We all want the dream location. The perfect alley. The perfect house. The perfect diner. But most of the time, we do not get that.

So we shape what we have.

A normal office can become a police station with the right blocking. A quiet street can feel dangerous with the right lens and sound design. A warehouse can turn into almost anything if you control the light.

That is what good production design does. It does not always build the world from scratch. Sometimes it hides the wrong stuff and points our eyes toward the right stuff.

Think like a location scout

When we scout, we should not only ask, “Does this place look good?”

We should ask better questions:

Can we control the sound here?

Where does the sun land?

Can we shoot wide without seeing something that breaks the story?

Can this place pass for somewhere else?

Can actors move through it in a way that feels alive?

That is where the real movie starts to show up.

Big films and small films use the same muscle

A studio action film may have traffic control, stunt teams, and city permits. We may have three crew members, two lights, and one borrowed location.

But the core job is the same. We are all trying to guide the audience’s eye.

If Melbourne can become New York, your local street can become part of your story too. The trick is not money alone. The trick is taste, planning, and knowing what to leave out of the frame.

What we are taking into our next shoot

This week’s note is simple: scout for story, not just looks.

Find places with texture. Find places with depth. Find places that give your actors something to do. Then use camera, light, sound, wardrobe, and art direction to sell the lie.

That is filmmaking. We build a world, one frame at a time.
