Blackmagic Hits Big

The News

Blackmagic Design announced that its cameras, post tools, live production gear, and DaVinci Resolve workflow were used on more than 50 summer film releases in 2026.

The list includes major titles like Billie Eilish: Hit Me Hard and Soft, Backrooms, Obsession, Resident Evil, Project Hail Mary, Spider-Man: Brand New Day, and Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu.

That is not a small gear flex. That is a signal. The tools that used to feel indie, scrappy, or side-stage are now sitting inside major studio pipelines.

Why This Matters

For years, a lot of filmmakers saw Blackmagic as the budget-friendly option. Great image, weird body, strong color, lower price. That was the pitch.

Now the story is bigger. We are seeing Blackmagic live in the full chain: camera, dailies, LUTs, monitoring, routing, editing, finishing, and final color. That matters because modern filmmaking is not just about what camera is on set. It is about how fast your footage moves, how clean your color pipeline is, and how well your team can stay in sync.

On Billie Eilish: Hit Me Hard and Soft, the reported gear list included the Blackmagic PYXIS 6K, URSA Broadcast G2, Micro Studio Camera 4K G2, Cloud Store, HyperDeck, ATEM switchers, and Teranex converters. That is a full production ecosystem, not just a camera body.

The Bigger Shift

The real story is workflow. A camera is only one piece of the machine.

On set, DITs are building looks, making dailies, checking signals, moving files, and helping protect the director and DP's intent. In post, colorists are carrying that look across the finish line. When one toolset can touch all of that, crews save time and reduce friction.

This is why DaVinci Resolve keeps showing up in these lists. It is not just color software anymore. It is used for dailies, conform, online, finishing, reference exports, and full post pipelines. That makes it useful for massive productions and lean crews alike.

What We Can Learn From It

For small teams like us, the lesson is not, “Buy what the studios buy.” That is too simple.

The real lesson is this: build a workflow before you build a gear list.

If you are making a commercial, short film, doc, or branded piece, ask better questions up front. How will we back up media? Who owns the LUT? Where does audio live? How fast do we need dailies? Are we editing, coloring, and finishing in the same app? Can the client review cuts without slowing the team down?

That stuff feels boring until it saves the shoot.

Our Take

We love when high-end tools become more open. It means more filmmakers can get close to the same pipeline language used on larger sets.

But gear does not make the film. Process does. Taste does. A clear plan does.

Blackmagic showing up on more than 50 summer films tells us the industry is hungry for flexible tools that can scale. A tiny crew can use the same core software as a studio feature. A DIT can carry a look from set to post. A director can trust that what we saw on the day is closer to what we will see in the final grade.

That is good news for filmmakers.

Not because one brand won the week. Because the gap between “small crew” and “big pipeline” keeps getting smaller.
